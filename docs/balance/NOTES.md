# Balance notes

Hand-written. `report.md` alongside is generated by the bot and gets overwritten each run.

## Targets (first guess, to confirm in play)

- A new player's first profitable trade happens on day one.
- First upgrade after about 20–30 minutes of real play: roughly 10–15 trips at about 2 minutes each, including flying and trading.
- No single route should earn more than about 1.5× the next best for long. Repeating one route should wear its margin down.
- Hand-flying the dock should feel worth it, but never be required.

## M1 bot run (180 game days, greedy, always pays the tug)

- **Day-one trades exist** because the economy warms up for 60 days before the start. Before that change, every market started at target stock and nothing was profitable.
- **Credits:** 5,000 → about 19,000 by day 10. The first 20 t container was fitted around day 39, and the bot ended near 70,000 at day 180 after buying five upgrades.
- **Routes:**
  - The top earners are Trojan Yards → The Kernel (habitat modules, about 7,600 per leg) and Kibo Ring → Clarke Exchange (electronics, food and machine parts, 1-day hops).
  - Clarke Exchange's electronics demand was cut (target 30 → 20, use 1.5 → 0.8 t/day) to stop it dominating.
- **Empty backhauls are common.** Clarke Exchange and The Kernel produce nothing, so the trips home are empty. This is the biggest gap in the loop.

## With NPC traffic (22 ships)

- NPCs now carry real goods, so the abstract background traffic was reduced (`relaxation_per_day` 0.12 → 0.06).
- That setting is the most sensitive knob in the economy:

  | relaxation_per_day | Credits at day 180 | First upgrade |
  |---|---|---|
  | 0.05 | 160k | day 6 |
  | 0.06 | ~78k | day 11–17 |
  | 0.065 | 59k | day 55 |
  | 0.08 | 39k | day 62 |

- **First version bug:** route NPCs delivered full loads whatever the destination needed. They flooded Kibo Ring with helium-3, so the bot made 487k carrying it *backwards*. Routes now deliver only to the destination's shortfall.
- **Noise:** NPC choices make bot results vary from run to run. Average several seeds before trusting small differences. That's a TODO for the bot.
- **Competition shows:** the bot sometimes loses money on a leg because an NPC got there first.

## Eight places, 26 NPCs, multi-seed bot (latest)

- The bot now runs 5 seeds and reports the spread.
- With 2 new places and 2 new fleets, pacing slowed: first upgrade on a median of day 33.
- Retuned: `relaxation_per_day` 0.06 → 0.055 and start credits 5,000 → 10,000. Early play was limited by cash, not cargo space.
- **Result:** median first upgrade on day 16 (target 11–17), mean 84k credits at day 180.
- The spread is wide (day 5–18), which is fine for now.
- The bot needs 15,000 credits in reserve on top of the module price before it upgrades, so a player can upgrade a bit earlier than it does.
- Clarke Exchange now exports salvaged metals, and The Kernel exports food, so fewer return legs are empty.

## Review fixes and projects

- Trade prices are now the **exact integral** of the price curve.
  - The old midpoint price let you buy big and sell back in slices for profit, up to +26k credits at one station.
  - Every review defect has a regression test in `tests/run_tests.gd` (`test_review_regressions`).
- The sim now advances in economy-step chunks, so tick size no longer changes the world (tested).
- Megaprojects add demand. Bot: mean 96k credits at day 180, median first upgrade on day 10, slightly fast against the 11–17 target. Watch it in play before tuning.

## Information economy

- The player no longer sees other ports' live prices. They see stale knowledge plus paid tips of varying honesty.
- The balance bot (and NPC traders) still trade on perfect live information. Its numbers are now an *upper* bound on what an informed player earns, not a floor.
- **TODO:** a bot mode that trades on its own knowledge plus tips, to measure what tips are worth.

## Docking

- A scripted pilot (`view/flight/autopilot.gd`, run with `-- --dock-trial` and part of `validate.ps1`) uses only player controls. It docks at all 8 stations from the real spawn in about 195–220 s, with no refusals.
- The hard part for people was technique nobody was taught: face down the axis, *then* strafe onto it.
- Added:
  - co-pilot prompts and an axis display
  - kinder tolerances: 1.5 m/s, 15°, 20° roll, 5 m capture
  - a 15k docking computer
  - the tug on credit
  - a Buy max reserve toggle
- Watch: is 3 minutes per hand-docking too long? A shorter spawn distance (600 m now) or faster safe-approach speeds would shorten it.

## Curved transfers

- Smooth-thrust paths in the rotating frame took about 22% longer than the old flip-and-burn.
- Pathfinder thrust was raised to compensate: Mk1 700 → 1000 N, Mk2 1200 → 1700 N. Trips from Kibo are now 2.6–2.8 days to the Moon and L-points, and 1 day to GEO.
- Bot: median first upgrade on day 10, mean about 100k at day 180 (one seed is an outlier at 25k).

## Ship power (Oct 2026)

Re-ran the bot (`days=60 upgrade=1`, seeds 1-5) on main and on the power branch: identical output (mean 29,086 cr, median first upgrade day 28.7). Power has no price, fuel or trip-time effect. The only link to the economy is that a survey or mining job waits while its sensors or rig are shed, which needs the reactor to be broken or the battery flat, and the bot never gets there.

Findings:
- A Mule parked with a cold reactor runs a deficit beyond about 1.6 AU: -6.4 kW at Psyche (about 94 h to empty), -8.6 kW at Hektor (about 70 h). Starting a site job lights the reactor, so only parking is affected.
- Watch: is 3-4 days of battery long enough once players wait at far sites under big time compression? If it is annoying, raise `battery_kwh` on `cmd_pod_basic` (600) or add a battery module.

## Detection and stealth (2026-10-08)

Without the coating or sink, the heat channel falls from about 12,300 km to about 6,800 km. The solar wings matter little in transit, where the reactor runs. The bot never stows.

## The co-pilot's estimate (2026-10-12)

- Boards and contract deadlines now use `sim/trip_estimate.gd`, which flies the routes the planner would offer, instead of the quick gravity-free sum.
- Measured first (Mule, full tanks, 5 ports × every destination × 3 dates, 300 trips):
  - The quick sum was never pessimistic. Near Earth and the Moon the fastest flyable route took 1.3 to 2.8 times as long as it said (for example, Trojan Yards to the Farside Array: 2.9 days quick, 6.4 days flown).
  - Earth-Mars, 11 of the 300 trips said "doable" when no flyable route was affordable.
  - Deadlines (quick time × slack 1.08 to 3.0) were often impossible even with an empty hold.
- After: the estimate matched the fastest affordable plotted route on all 300 trips. It takes 0.25 s on average and 0.76 s at worst per trip, remembered for the hour.
- Deadlines get longer where gravity makes trips slower, and pay rises with them (pay is per day of the reference trip). Expect cislunar courier jobs to pay noticeably more per job but about the same per day flown.
- Balance bot re-run (5 seeds, 180 days, upgrade=1): identical, seed for seed (mean 105,630 cr). The bot only takes a job bound where its trade is already going, and in these runs it saw none (no jobs_seen in its ECON rows, before or after), so it doesn't see the change. Worth a hand-played check of courier income.

## Goods for the 2060s (2026-10-27)

- Changed data: `goods.json` goes from 14 goods to 28 (high tech, printer feedstocks, gene stock, Earth luxuries, frontier exports), with flows at every place in `places.json`. - **The start is faster and steadier.** The first upgrade comes on day 6.7–6.9 in four seeds out of five (15.0 in the fifth), against 5.9–37.2 before. Day one has more good trades near Earth.
- **The mean is lower, but within the noise.** Seeds vary by a factor of five either way, so 83k against 106k over five seeds is not a clear change. More seeds would settle it.
- **Routes are far more varied.** Seed 1 used 35 route-and-good pairs. The top earners are compute cores into Trojan Yards (its drone works eat them), orbital pharmaceuticals from the Kernel and Kalpana One to Clarke Exchange and Kibo Ring, and storage lattices from Kalpana One. No route dominates: the best repeated one (Kalpana One to Clarke, pharmaceuticals) made 31k over three legs.

- **The bot barely touches the luxuries.** Their targets are small (a few tonnes), so a greedy single-good trader finds little volume. The widest margins at start are luxuries to the far ports (wine from Ares Ring to Valhalla, gene stock to Tharsis, both about 225% of base), which the bot doesn't reach. Worth a hand-played check: a long-haul ship with mixed small loads of luxuries may now be the best way to earn in the outer system.
- No tuning yet. Watch for orbital pharmaceuticals becoming the one early route everyone runs.
